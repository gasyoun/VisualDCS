## Why this gate existed

H6995's NCC MSS census could not compute its `Skandapurāṇa` canary: the bodies
export (`SktNewCatalogus_Catalogorum_combined.txt` → `ncc.jsonl`, 152,526 rows)
carries **no** IAST title beginning `Sk`/`Sl`/`Sm`/`Sn`/`So`/`Sr` and no
Devanagari title beginning `स्क`, although `Skandapur…` is referenced 1,275 times
inside entry bodies. Two readings were on the table:

1. the fascicles exist in the 2022 database but were dropped from the export (**recoverable**), or
2. NCC had not published past that page, so `स्क…` does not exist yet (**not recoverable now**).

The archive is the discriminator. This document settles it with numbers.

## 4. Where the database stops, and why that is not a sort artifact

| fact | value |
|---|---|
| volumes present | 1–39 (39 volumes) |
| last volume's last page | 39 → p. **378** |
| last record | `सुहोदित · Suhodita · 39_378_b_4090` |
| pagination-order violations `(vol,page,seq)` | **2** in 152,526 records |
| Devanagari codepoint-order violations | **8,551** |

Two conclusions, and the second is why H7078 warned against assuming an order:

- The table is in **strict catalogue order** (2 violations across the whole
  file), and the last record *is* the maximum pagination present. There is no
  out-of-sequence tail in which the block could be filed.
- The Devanagari column is **not** a codepoint sort (8,551 violations), so
  "the `स्क` block would come next alphabetically" is not a safe inference, and a
  recovered block would have to be placed by pagination — never appended, never
  codepoint-inserted. (Moot here: there is nothing to place.)

The three `स्`-conjunct headwords that exist (`स्तुद्य्।`, `स्वय्ं_बोध_…`, `स्।_वेङ्कटराम_…`)
sit at volumes 29 and 31 — inside the sequence, not after the end. They are
ordinary odd titles, not evidence of a misplaced block.

## What this proves, and what it does not

**Proves.** The bundled 2022 database contains no `स्क` headword, in any of its
seven title tables, under literal or punctuation-insensitive matching; the
database is internally complete to vol 39 p. 378 with its records in catalogue
order; and the committed export is an exact serialization of it. The gate-1
question — *are the fascicles in the archive but missing from the export?* — is
answered **no**.

**Does not prove.** That NCC has never published the block. The database is
2022-vintage and internally consistent at its frontier; absence in a 2022
snapshot says nothing about a fascicle published after it. What would settle the
recoverability question outright:

1. an NCC fascicle covering the `स` volume past p. 378, or
2. a **current** NCC desktop-database export, to re-run this probe against.

Both are acquisitions, not analyses — this gate closes as *not recoverable from
what we hold*.
