## Denominators (every count below names its set)

- **Dictionary side:** 19,177 distinct final members after orthographic normalization, restricted
  to classes `UTTARAPADA` + `KRT_STEM_MEMBER`. The bound taddhita suffixes (`-tva`, `-tā`, `-vat`
  …) are **not** members and were excluded upstream in issue15 — see its
  [README](https://github.com/gasyoun/MWderivations/blob/master/issue15/README.md). `mw_first_members`
  is a **type count** of distinct first members, never a token count.
- **Corpus side:** 399,096 distinct compound word-forms (the `cmps.csv` universe), of which 168,481
  carry a measured DCS token frequency (`names.csv`); 21,958 distinct final members.
  `corpus_compounds` counts distinct word-forms ending in a member; `corpus_tokens` sums DCS
  frequency over the freq-annotated subset (a form outside it adds to the form-count, 0 tokens).

## Two encoding traps had to be folded — and two morphological ones deliberately were not

A naïve string join matches only **29 %** of MW finals, almost all of the miss being artefacts, not
absences. Verified on a codepoint census before trusting anything:

- **Folded** (pure orthography, both sides): anusvāra (MW `ṃ` U+1E43 vs DCS `ṁ` U+1E41), avagraha
  (`'bja` = elided *abja*), and MW's markup characters `@` / `-`. This is the `okey()` join key.
- **Not folded** (morphology — folding would manufacture matches): final vowel length / gender
  (`sena` vs corpus `senā`; `vatī` vs `vat`; `phalā` vs `phala`) and junction sandhi (`cchada` vs
  *chada*, `ṣṭha` vs *stha*). These are diagnosed as `form_variant`, **never** asserted absent.

## Finding 1 — dictionary and corpus are productive over the *same* finals but with *disjoint* first members

The strongest signal is not in the absences but in the 6,249 finals **both** sides attest. There the
first-member vocabularies barely overlap:

- **median Jaccard of the two first-member sets = 0.00** (mean 0.10);
- **3,526 of 6,249 matched finals (56 %) share zero first members** with the corpus at all.

So even where MW and the DCS corpus agree that "-U" is a live compound head, they overwhelmingly
disagree about *which* words sit in front of it. Two clean specimens:

| final | mw_first_members | corpus_first_members | overlap | corpus_tokens |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `-indra` (lord-of) | 2 | 286 | **0** | 3,072 |
| `-ādi` (and-so-on) | 13 | 2,126 | 5 | 5,323 |
| `-pati` (master-of) | 358 | 200 | 90 | 1,308 |

`-pati` is the well-behaved case (large productive final, 90 shared first members). `-indra` and
`-ādi` are the norm: the corpus productivity is one to two orders of magnitude larger than the
dictionary's, and they intersect in almost nothing. **This is "productive in the dictionary vs
attested in the corpus" made concrete at the level of the individual final member** — the thing
neither side records alone.

## Direction A — dictionary-only (corpus-unattested) final members

**Framing first, honestly.** Corpus-unattested is **not** the same as ghost-word. The
SanskritLexicography ghost-word work already established that the pwg/mw "ghost" residue is dominated
by corpus gaps and a legitimate *kośa-only lexical stratum* (Rājanighaṇṭu / Trikāṇḍaśeṣa / Amara
formations, MS-catalogue proper nouns), not spurious words —
[FINDINGS §86](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/FINDINGS.md) further showed
the grammarians' canonical samāsa examples are themselves largely corpus-unattested (8/58). Exact
normalized match is a **lower bound** on attestation, so the 10,387 are candidates, not verdicts.

And the stratum is overwhelmingly low-productivity: **8,892 of 10,387 (86 %) are MW hapax finals**
(a single first member even inside MW), median `mw_first_members` = 1. Only **50** absent finals have
`mw_first_members ≥ 10` — the genuinely-productive-yet-corpus-absent head, which sorts into recognisable
lexicographic classes:

| class | examples (mw_first) | reading |
|---|---|---|
| work-title / *nāma* formations (kośa tradition) | `puṣpikā` (36), `ratnāvalī` (29), `muktāvalī` (22), `prakāśikā` (19), `campū` (17), `bhaṭṭīya` (12) | titles of texts/anthologies — dictionary head-words, not corpus tokens |
| deverbal-participle finals DCS does not segment | `baddha` (33), `cyuta` (25), `varjita` (24), `pūta` (22), `gṛhīta` (19), `pālita` (12), `saṃyukta` (12) | MW lists `X-baddha` compounds; DCS keeps the participle unsegmented / lemmatised |
| inflected adverbial finals | `pūrvakam` (12), `taram` (14), `kāram` (18) | MW cites the frozen `-am` adverbial; DCS lemmatises to the stem |

**Excluded from the claim (residual MW-index split artefacts, ~40 of the head):** finals beginning
`ṃ-` (`ṃgama` 27, `ṃjaya` 18, `ṃdhara` 18) or with a stranded `r`/`ṛ` (`rṣi` 18 = *ṛṣi*, `rtu` 13 =
*ṛtu*, `rca` 13 = *ṛca*) are cases where the pūrvapada's final segment bled into the uttarapada key
in the source index. They are not real dictionary-only finals and are flagged, not headlined.

## Direction B — corpus-frequent, MW-thin final members

The mirror divergence: final members that dominate real text but MW's derivation markup under-lists.
Ranked by DCS tokens (which auto-suppresses the corpus's own junk head, below) among finals with
`mw_first_members ≤ 20`:

| final | corpus_tokens | corpus_first_members | mw_first_members | reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `-ādi` | 5,323 | 2,126 | 13 | "X and the rest" — the corpus's chief enumeration device |
| `-indra` | 3,072 | 286 | 2 | "lord/chief of" (*narendra*, *devendra*) |
| `-ātman` | 2,346 | 1,216 | 12 | reflexive "-self / consisting of" |
| `-ṛṣi` | 1,690 | 233 | 14 | "seer" (*maharṣi*, *rājarṣi*) |
| `-ākhyā` | 1,288 | 1,137 | 2 | "named X" — productive naming head |
| `-agni` | 1,157 | 839 | 10 | "fire of" |
| `-ādya` | 1,093 | 995 | 1 | "beginning with" (enumeration, like `-ādi`) |
| `-ābha` | 727 | 572 | **0** | "resembling / -like" (comparison bahuvrīhi) |
| `-uttama` | 685 | 263 | 2 | "best of" |
| `-vyāghra` | 673 | 61 | 9 | "tiger-among-" = pre-eminent |
| `-adhipa` | 589 | 121 | 4 | "overlord of" |

The semantic pattern is sharp: the corpus-dominant-yet-MW-thin finals are the **productive
enumeration, comparison, and "lord/best/pre-eminent-of" heads** (`-ādi`, `-ādya`, `-ābha`, `-uttama`,
`-vyāghra`, `-indra`, `-adhipa`). These are exactly the compositional patterns a reader meets on the
first page of any epic and a derivation-oriented dictionary is least inclined to enumerate.

## The corpus has its own junk head — the mirror of MW's taddhita head

Trap #1 of the dictionary side (bound taddhita suffixes crowding the top) has an exact corpus-side
twin: DCS over-segments enclitics, pronoun stems, and bare verb roots as compound members. Ranked by
compound-**form** count, the corpus head is almost entirely non-lexical — and the token column exposes
it, because these split-forms never lexicalise:

| corpus final | forms | tokens | kind |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `ca` | 11,057 | **2** | enclitic "and" |
| `eva` | 5,660 | 0 | particle |
| `idam` / `tad` | 3,926 / 3,898 | 0 / 54 | pronoun stems |
| `kṛ` / `as` / `bhū` / `gam` | 3,870 / 3,597 / 3,060 / 2,542 | 3 / 3 / 4 / 2 | bare verb roots |
| `tva` / `tā` | 3,546 / 1,755 | 4,830 / 2,758 | **taddhita suffixes MW excluded** — high tokens, so ranking by tokens alone does not drop them; explicit stoplist required |

This is why Direction B is ranked by tokens **and** filtered by an explicit stoplist (particles +
pronoun stems + bare roots + the taddhita suffixes): tokens remove the particles and roots for free,
but the abstract-noun suffixes `-tva`/`-tā` are genuinely high-frequency and must be named and set
aside by hand, exactly as issue15 set them aside on the dictionary side.

## Ceiling / discipline

- **`absent` is a lower bound on real absence.** Normalized exact match misses variant spelling,
  further sandhi, and any segmentation DCS performs differently. Treat the 10,387 as candidates.
- **Form differences are reported, not folded.** 1,289 `form_variant` finals (`sena`/`senā`,
  `vatī`/`vat`) are morphological citation-form mismatches; merging them would inflate the join and
  hide a real MW-vs-DCS lemmatisation difference.
- **No temporal (early/late) cut was made.** The per-period frequency vectors in `names.csv` have
  **no documented column→period mapping** (stated in the Kompozity
  [README](https://github.com/gasyoun/VisualDCS/blob/main/derived-data/Kompozity/README.md)); assigning
  early/late productivity without that labelling would be guesswork, so it is explicitly left undone —
  not silently skipped.
- **DCS segmentation policy drives much of Direction A.** The participle and adverbial classes are
  absent because DCS does not segment those compounds, not because the words are unknown to the corpus.
