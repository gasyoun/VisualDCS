### The pinned pair

The DCS lemma_id **48482** (`bhagavat`, 3,238 tokens) is tagged **ADJ**, which is exactly why it fell outside Sangram G2's `upos='NOUN'` universe and could not be classified by H3984. This pass reaches it. MW indexes भगवत् under `162249,162473,162474,162493,162494,162495,162496,162497,162498` and भगवन्त् under — (verdict: `at_only`). PWG indexes भगवत् under `53806,80057` and भगवन्त् under `53806,80057` (verdict: `one_lexeme_two_spellings`). Combined verdict: **`one_lexeme_two_spellings`**. The two spellings resolve to the **same entry ids**, so the lexicon itself states that `bhagavat` and `bhagavant` are one lexeme written two ways. This is a declaration in the dictionary, not a character rule inferred from the endings — the distinction the H1472 fence exists to protect.

### The unresolved residual — why it stays pooled

**659 lemma_ids / 1,203 ADJ tokens (3.27 % of the ADJ mass)** have no witness in either MW or PWG under either spelling, and are left pooled — never split on a character rule.

- **528 lemma_ids** (909 tokens) carry the productive `-vat`/`-mat`/`-vant`/`-mant` possessive shape.
- **123 lemma_ids** (282 tokens) have an augmented-participle shape (`a-` + bare `-at`).
- **449 lemma_ids** are single-token hapax; the largest row in the whole tail is 36 tokens.

The tail is dominated by -vat/-mat possessive derivatives and by single-token hapax compounds: the suffix is fully productive, so the corpus carries ad-hoc derivatives no lexicon indexes as headwords. That is the expected behaviour of an external-signal method, not a defect in it -- these stay pooled until a third lexicon is added.

_DCS tags every one of these lemma_ids `adj` in `lemma.grammar` and carries no VERB token for any of them, so the participle-looking rows are a SHAPE observation, not a corpus-attested part of speech._
