Sanskrit compound lexicography · Monier-Williams × DCS · H1328

Productive in the dictionary,
attested in the corpus

A dictionary records which words a lexicographer chose to list before a compound's final member. A corpus records which compounds a civilisation actually wrote. Joining Monier-Williams' derivation index to the DCS text corpus on the compound final member shows where those two facts part ways — and they part ways almost completely.

Join of the MWderivations issue-15 uttarapada index (19,177 kept final members) against DCS Kompozity (399,096 compound word-forms, 168,481 with measured frequency). Full method + report: uttarapada_dict_vs_corpus_divergence.md. Analysis Opus 4.8.

01

The same final members, filled with different words

Each dot is one compound final member that both the dictionary and the corpus treat as productive (6,249 of them). Its horizontal position is how many distinct first-members Monier-Williams lists; its vertical position is how many the corpus attests. Colour is agreement — how many of those first-members the two sides actually share.

Both axes are logarithmic. The cloud rises — dictionary-productive finals do tend to be corpus-attested (Spearman ρ = +0.50). But it is overwhelmingly pale: even where both sides are productive, they populate the final with almost disjoint vocabularies. indra "lord-of" sits high (286 corpus first-members) and far left (2 in the dictionary) with zero shared; ādi "and-the-rest" the same. Only the well-worn pati, ja, rūpa approach the diagonal of agreement.

The two divergences

The join names them in both directions: finals the dictionary lists richly but the corpus barely writes, and finals the corpus writes constantly but the dictionary's markup under-lists.

05

Both sides carry a junk head — and they mirror each other

A raw ranking is meaningless until each side's non-lexical top is removed. The measurement's honesty rests on filtering both.

Dictionary head — bound suffixes

Monier-Williams' reverse index is topped by taddhita suffixes that are not compound members at all. Excluded upstream by word-class.

Corpus head — particles & roots

DCS over-segments enclitics, pronoun stems and bare roots as "members". The token column exposes them: ca "and" heads 11,057 forms but recurs twice.

Reading discipline. Two orthographic mismatches between the sides are folded (anusvāra ṃ vs ṁ; MW markup @/-/leading avagraha) — a naïve join matches only 29%. Vowel-length, gender and junction-sandhi differences (sena/senā, cchada/chada) are not folded — 1,289 diagnosed as form-variants, never asserted absent. The early/late temporal cut is deliberately skipped: the corpus's per-period columns carry no documented period labels, so it would be guesswork.
